# Street Political Economy: Tenement Dynamics & Community Reality

**Target Application:** `LAWMA-360-customer-app` (Resident & Compound PWA/USSD)  
**Domain Focus:** Tenancy Politics, Cash Surcharges, and Informal Dumping Culture

---

## 1. The Tenement Compound (*Face-Me-I-Face-You*) Micro-Society
- **The Compound Chairman:** In multi-tenant houses, an informal "compound chairman" (often the longest-residing tenant or caretaker) collects communal contributions. This person often pockets a percentage of utility money, creating friction with landlords.
- **Digital Disintermediation:** By allowing each tenant to pay directly to their flat sub-account via their own phone, the app dismantles the corrupt caretaker intermediary while guaranteeing payment accuracy.

## 2. The POS Agent Micro-Economy
- **The Cash Crunch Factor:** Many informal workers in Lagos receive cash wages and do not use mobile banking apps. They rely on neighborhood Point-of-Sale (POS) agents.
- **Surcharge Barrier:** POS agents charge ₦100 to ₦200 per cash deposit. If paying a ₦600 waste bill incurs a ₦200 agent fee, the citizen refuses to pay.
- **Mitigation:** The system partners with major agent networks (e.g. Moniepoint, OPay) to subsidize agent fees on municipal waste payments, making cash bill settlement zero-fee at neighborhood kiosks.

## 3. The ₦200 Midnight Cart Pusher Dynamic
- **Why Canals Get Blocked:** When a formal PSP fails to collect for two weeks, bins overflow and rot. Residents hire illegal cart pushers for ₦200 to remove the filth. Under cover of darkness or heavy rainfall, cart pushers dump the waste into municipal stormwater canals.
- **System Cure:** The Saturday-noon auto-protection ensures that missed pickups trigger emergency intervention compactors within 24 hours, stopping the desperation cycle that feeds canal dumping.
